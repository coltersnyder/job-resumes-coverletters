\textbf{Re: Launch Systems \& Real-Time Controls Software Engineer 2, Ground Software Fluids (Req.\ 2255)}

Dear Hiring Team,

Before a ULA rocket flies, software has to load it with cryogenic propellant safely and on time,
through manufacturing, preflight test, and countdown. I would like to help write and maintain that
software. I am a software engineer at Innovative Scientific Solutions, where I develop ITAR- and
CUI-controlled nuclear weapons effects simulation platforms for defense organizations. I hold an
M.S.\ in Computer Science from the Colorado School of Mines, and I would bring two years of
experience building software whose results people rely on.

At ISSI I work across the full lifecycle on a small agile team and have led development of several
parts of our codebase. I refine requirements, design object-oriented architectures, document them in
UML and design documents, implement features in Python and Rust on Linux, and verify them with
automated tests in GitLab CI/CD with SonarQube analysis. I spearheaded our internal
performance-benchmarking tool, a Python command-line application with a GUI front end that drives our
calculation services effect by effect, alone and in combination, and charts the results. Much of my work is porting legacy physics
models from Fortran and Python to Rust without changing their numerical behavior. Both have taught
me to treat every change as something that must be measured and proven correct, not just made to
compile.

What draws me to this role in particular is where software meets hardware. Outside work I program
in C, design FPGA logic in Verilog, and write 6502 and Z80 assembly, where timing and the physical behavior of the
hardware matter as much as the code. My graduate coursework in cyber-physical systems security and
IoT security focused on how software that commands physical equipment can fail or be attacked, and
how to design against that. Commanding propulsion and electrical ground support equipment under hard
real-time requirements is the professional version of the problems I seek out on my own time, and I
am eager to deepen my work in C++ and real-time Linux on systems where it counts.

The role also calls for presenting designs and working closely with a range of stakeholders. At ISSI
I take part in technical meetings with our government customers, and I co-taught CSCI 220: Data
Structures and Algorithms in C++ to 120 students and taught another section of 90. Teaching taught me
to explain technical designs clearly to audiences with very different backgrounds. I am comfortable with a fail-fast culture: I would rather find a problem on
my bench or in a test run than on the pad.

Space has fascinated me for as long as I can remember, and contributing to ULA's record of mission
success here in Colorado, close to my home in Lakewood, is an opportunity I would be proud to take.
I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can support the Ground Software Fluids team. Thank you
for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,

Colter Snyder
